Tim Berners-Lee and the World Wide Web

Tim Berners-Lee's Early Research

Tim Berners-Lee studied physics and worked at CERN, where scientists needed a better way to share information. In 1989, he proposed the World Wide Web to help people access and connect information across different computers.

How the Web Is Used Today

Today, the World Wide Web is used for much more than sharing scientific research. People use it to communicate, study, shop, watch videos, read news and find information. Businesses and schools also use websites to reach people around the world.

Comparing the Two

Berners-Lee's original idea focused on making information easier for researchers to share. The web still does this today, but it has grown into a tool used in almost every part of daily life. The main difference is how widely it is now used, while sharing information remains an important purpose.
